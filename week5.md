## Spiritual Progression Portfolio: Comprehensive Review & Realignment Guide

### Christlike Attributes & Personal Warning Signs Analysis

#### A. Core Attributes Under Development

**Charity (Moroni 7:45)**: Placing focus on exhibiting charity by treating
colleagues and clients with respect while showing restraint and
resilience during high-pressure system outages or technical discussions.
The Love that Christ embodies is displayed through blameless
communication and treating everyone with respect while avoiding
contention .

**Diligence (D&C 64:33-34; Ecclesiastes 9:10)**: Writing clean and testable
code while monitoring systems carefully and establishing well-written
documentations proves our diligence and prepares us for the Lord's
blessings. Understanding that Heavenly Father respects and blesses our
efforts reminds me that my hard work counts.

#### B. Personal Warning Signs of Spiritual and Emotional Drift

The DevOps and cloud infrastructure professional environment can be very
fast paced. Therefore, there is high risk of burnout and cognitive
overload if not properly managed. The indicators below would serve as a
warning for me to adjust when they occur:

**Skipping Morning Devotionals**: Neglecting early morning Bible study and
prayer leaves my mind unanchored, replacing divine focus with anxiety
and scattered thinking.

**Late-Night Work & Sleep Deprivation**: Pushing through complex
configurations late into the night at the expense of necessary rest
directly degrades cognitive stamina.

**Heightened Irritability**: When exhausted and spirituality is depleted,
impatience can surface quickly during cross-functional meetings, code
reviews or unexpected operational disruptions.

#### C. Deliberate Strategies for Realignment

It is important to identify the methods for restoring cognitive balance
when at the brink of burnout. The steps below would help restore balance
before operational friction compromises relationships or work quality:

**Enforcing D&C 88:124**: Resetting my physical sleep schedule by sleeping
early and creating a bedtime alarm to eliminate fatigue. Creating an
alarm for end of workday to avoid overtime and rising early to
invigorate the body and mind.

**Upholding Morning Scripture Study & Prayer**: Protecting the first hour of
the day for prayer and scripture study to dissolve anxiety and regain
mental clarity.

**Uplifting Music**: Using hymns to quiet an anxious mind, reset emotional
composure and invite the Spirit back into the workspace.

**Strict Sabbath Observance**: Unplugging completely from deployment
dashboards, work chat boards, cloud consoles and technical tasks on
Sunday to honor covenants, renew perspective and achieve genuine
spiritual renewal.

### IT Portfolio Comprehensive Review and Reflection

My professional portfolio is intended to show both my professional
growth and my spiritual progression. It represents my journey from a
humanities student to becoming an IT professional while also showing the
values that are important to me as a follower of Jesus Christ. Through
this portfolio, I want to demonstrate how my IT abilities have developed
through different projects and how my spiritual values influence the
type of professional I am becoming.

Before this capstone, I had never created a professional portfolio. My
portfolio initially existed only as a document that I submitted for
assignments. I began developing it in Week 2 and by Week 3, It moved
online. Now in Week 5, each weekly assignment has helped me develop a
different part of the portfolio. So far, I have documented scriptures,
conference messages, speeches and personal experiences that have
strengthened my beliefs in charity, diligence and personal agency. I
have also included IT projects I have completed and examples of how I
improved some of my work.

One important feedback I received from my grader was to add tabs that
would allow users to move easily from one week to another. I implemented
this feedback by organizing the tasks according to their specific weeks.
After making the change, I realized that the portfolio became easier to
navigate and read. This experience also reminded me of the importance of
accepting feedback and using it to improve my work.

After reviewing the portfolio, I believe one of its strengths is the
content. The different sections are explanatory and provide information
about my experiences and growth. However, I also noticed that the
portfolio currently feels like separate weekly assignments rather than
one complete portfolio. I understand that each weekly task helps me
build and understand a different part of the final portfolio. For the
final version, I want to bring the strongest parts of these assignments
together so that they tell one clear story about my professional and
spiritual development.

This capstone has especially helped me understand that my spirituality
and profession are not separate. As Elder David A. Bednar has taught,
they can be compared to strands that are intertwined together. I am
learning that I should not rely only on my own understanding. I can
depend on Heavenly Father for guidance while still recognizing my
responsibility to work, learn, make decisions and do my part. Looking
back at the projects I have completed has also reminded me of how much I
have been able to accomplish through resilience.

Before finalizing my portfolio, I need to improve the project evidence,
résumé content, formatting and spiritual reflections. Formatting is
currently one of my biggest challenges because I am still becoming
familiar with Markdown. I also want my projects to provide stronger
evidence of my technical abilities and my spiritual reflections to show
more clearly how my beliefs influence my professional life. My goal is
for the final portfolio to present a cohesive picture of who I am, what
I can do, how far I have progressed and the values that will continue to
guide me in my IT career.

### Usability Testing and Portfolio Enhancements

I conducted a usability review of my professional portfolio to determine
how easily users could navigate the website, understand its organization
and find important information. I considered the portfolio from the
perspective of my instructor, potential employers and recruiters. One of
the main usability concerns was navigation. My grader previously
suggested adding tabs that would allow users to move easily from one
week to another. After reviewing the portfolio, I agreed that the weekly
assignments needed a clearer structure. I initially organized the
assignments according to their specific weeks, which made the portfolio
easier to navigate.

After further usability testing, I realized that organizing the entire
portfolio around weeks made it feel more like a collection of class
assignments than a professional portfolio. A recruiter or potential
employer may be more interested in quickly finding information about me,
my projects, resume, technical abilities and values instead of a layout
of class assignments. To improve this, I reorganized so that the main
sections contain both professional and spiritual information, while my
weekly assignments are grouped under a separate weekly reports section.
Creating a weekly-report.md page that serves as the main page for my
weekly progress. From this page, users can select individual weeks, such
as Week 4 or Week 5 and view the work completed during that period. This
keeps the weekly evidence required for my capstone without allowing it
to control the structure of the entire portfolio.

The usability test also showed that some areas still require
improvement. My project evidence needs to be more visual and easier to
evaluate, my résumé needs to better represent my current abilities and
some of my spiritual reflections can make stronger connections between
my beliefs and professional development. I also need to continue
improving formatting consistency as I become more familiar with
Markdown.

Overall, the usability testing helped me recognize the difference
between simply having good content and presenting that content
effectively. The changes I made improved navigation and created a
clearer separation between my academic progress and my finished
professional portfolio. My next step is to continue refining the
formatting, project presentation, resume and spiritual reflections so
that the final portfolio provides a cohesive representation of both my
professional and spiritual growth.

### Ethical Reflection

Reviewing my ethical dilemma helped me understand that making the right
ethical decision involves more than admitting a mistake. I still believe
the engineer was right to report the incident because honesty and
integrity should guide our decisions even when no one is watching.
However, the reevaluation taught me that honesty should also be followed
by immediate and appropriate action.

Instead of only reporting the mistake, engineers should take initiative
by starting containment, raising the appropriate incident ticket and
alerting everyone involved. Acting quickly could improve the chances of
recovering the lost files. This showed me that ethical responsibility
and technical competence work together. If I am responsible for other
people's data, I need the right technical knowledge to protect it and
respond appropriately when something goes wrong.

This dilemma also strengthened my understanding of personal agency.
Agency allows me to recognize my mistakes, take responsibility, act on
them and accept the consequences. During difficult professional
situations, prayer can also be an anchor and remind me that I can seek
Christ's guidance while still doing my part.

Ultimately, I want to become an honest and responsible IT professional
who takes accountability for her mistakes, responds appropriately and
learns from them.
